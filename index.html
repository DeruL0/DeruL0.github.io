    Shuyang Zhu · Youlong Li · Jizhao Liu · Bin Li*

    CTGS: Hybrid Gaussian Splatting for Compact Industrial CT Representation
    Shuyang Zhu* · Yukie Nagai

    • An industrial CT algorithm that converts CT volumes into a compact Gaussian Splatting representation.
    • Focuses on internal CT structures, including voids and variations in material density, and surface shape alignment to support downstream analysis such as surface extraction.
    • Uses a hybrid model of surface and bulk Gaussians to represent material boundaries and internal attenuation, respectively; file size can be reduced to about 1/35 of the original DICOM data in the best case, with extracted meshes achieving a normalized Chamfer-L1 of about 0.23–0.50%.

    Projects

    X-ray Projection-to-Mesh (Ongoing)
    Topology-Adaptive Surface Reconstruction

    • A pipeline that generates watertight surface meshes directly from X-ray projections, without reconstructing a volume first.
    • Topology can change during optimization: the mesh can split into several parts, grow through-holes, or open internal cavities, without knowing the object’s topology in advance.
    • Recovers the correct topology on every test shape. Surface error is about 0.1–0.15% of the bounding-box diagonal, and each shape fits in under a minute with native CUDA acceleration.

    Adaptive Octree CT Reconstruction from Sparse Projections (Ongoing)
    Compact Projection-Domain Volume Representation

    • A pipeline that reconstructs CT volumes directly from X-ray projections into a compact adaptive octree, for storing and analyzing large industrial CT scans.
    • Focuses on sharp material interfaces, thin gaps and accurate surface extraction under a fixed byte budget and sparse views: at 8 MB (232× smaller than the 1.98 GB voxel volume) it reaches 37.8 dB volume PSNR and 0.19-voxel mean surface distance, and loses only 0.55 dB when training views drop from 240 to 64.
    • Uses typed octree leaves (constant, trilinear, planar step) with exact CUDA ray integration, held-out-view refinement decisions and an edge-preserving regularizer; one run takes about 9 minutes on a laptop GPU.
